We screened 17 device makers.
The price already knew.

A 12-metric screen picked three winners. Valuing them asked a different question: what does today's price already assume? Two were priced for exactly the growth they promise. The third was priced for more than twice that.

How it works
Step 1 · The screen

17 companies, ranked on 12 checked metrics

Every metric is turned into a percentile rank (100 = best of the 17), averaged into five groups and weighted. The ranking was then re-run with 10,000 random sets of weights: the finalists are the companies that stay top 3 whatever the weights. Click any company to open it.

Not in the screen

Four of the 21 candidates failed a rule written before any data was looked at.

Step 2 · The valuation

Three finalists. 10,000 futures each.

Pick a finalist, move the assumptions, and run the simulation. Each run draws growth, margins, the discount rate and long-run growth at random within the ranges you set, values the company with a 10-year DCF, and counts how often it is worth more than its price.

Method

Rules first, data second

Every rule was written down before looking at results, every company's numbers were cross-checked against its own SEC filing, and every change of method is declared. The full log is in the repository README.
